## How the decision will be made

### Who decides

The superintendent makes a recommendation in November after reviewing data
and community feedback. The school board makes the final decision in a public
meeting in December.

### The process

PPS describes these steps (video 5):

1. Gather data on each school: enrollment, resource use, programs, and
   building condition.
2. Gather input from students, families, staff, and culturally specific
   communities.
3. Apply the PPS Racial Equity Lens.
4. Develop scenarios, meaning different ways to balance enrollment.
5. Present the scenarios to the board on October 6.
6. Hold more community engagement on the scenarios.
7. The superintendent recommends one plan in November.
8. The board votes in December.

### The guiding question

"How will the changes improve students' access to educational supports,
resources, and programming in safe and healthy learning environments?"
(video 4)

### Factors PPS says it will weigh

- **Enrollment.** The target is roughly 360 students for a K-5 school and
  roughly 500 for a K-8 or middle school (video 4).
- **Building condition and capacity.** When schools combine, PPS gives
  preference to "the building in better condition with better facilities"
  (video 4). The FAQ adds operating costs, building use, and earthquake risk.
- **Dual language immersion.** Some immersion programs may be consolidated to
  keep them strong (video 4). The FAQ says immersion, special education, and
  gifted programs are planned as preschool-through-12th-grade pathways and
  that every student will keep required services.
- **Geography.** PPS wants to keep families close to their schools and limit
  travel time (video 4).
- **Keeping classmates together.** When students move, PPS will try to move
  them with their classmates (video 4).
- **Equity.** Every plan is reviewed with the PPS Racial Equity Lens (FAQ,
  video 5).
- **Community and student input.** Results from the fall engagement go to the
  board before the December vote (video 6).

PPS repeatedly says no single measure decides the outcome. The April 13
letter says no decision will rest on one data point, "especially not
utilization alone." The FAQ says building condition and safety data will not
by themselves decide whether a school closes.

### What the scenarios can include

School closures, consolidations, boundary changes, program moves, and changes
to how buildings are used (FAQ, main page).

### What PPS says it is aiming for

The board chair describes the goal as stability, sustainability, safe and
healthy buildings, and a strong network of neighborhood schools with a balance
of educational options (video 3).
A "well-resourced" school, in his description, has:

- stable staffing with single-grade classrooms and teachers who are not split
  across buildings or roles
- a full range of enrichment, electives, athletics, and academic and
  intervention supports
- access to culturally specific partnerships, mental health and nursing
  supports, and family resources
- a safe, healthy, comfortable building

### The case PPS makes for acting now

- Enrollment has fallen for about a decade because of lower birth rates,
  families leaving Portland, and families choosing other schools (video 2).
- Funding follows enrollment, and PPS already made cuts this year with a
  significant shortfall expected next year (video 2).
- The board chair says 20% of schools are subsidizing the other 80% (video 3).
- Armstrong says doing nothing would lead to further program and staff
  reductions (video 1).
- PPS's example: a 350-student school and a 200-student school combined into
  one 550-student school could afford full-time art and PE and 22-student
  fourth-grade classes, instead of classes of 30 in one building and 14 in the
  other (video 2).

### After a decision

- Staffing changes follow union contracts and existing district processes
  (FAQ).
- A closed building gets a reuse plan with community input, following board
  policy on using or selling district property (FAQ).
- Boundary changes would consider where students live, school size, programs,
  transportation, and elementary-to-high-school feeder patterns (FAQ).

## How to give input

- Submit questions through the FAQ page form, or email Rightsizing@pps.net.
- RSVP for events at pps.net/rightsizing-rsvp.
- The main engagement channels are the districtwide family advisory group,
  community conversations for specific groups, student councils, and informal
  board open houses (video 6).
