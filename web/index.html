More background and timeline

On March 10, 2026, Superintendent Kimberlee Armstrong announced that Portland Public Schools (PPS) may close some number of its 74 elementary, K-8, middle, and alternative schools by fall 2027. The driver is a decade of declining enrollment (projected to fall another 14% by 2035-36) and a structural budget gap.

On October 5, 2026, PPS released two draft scenarios. Scenario A closes 14 schools and Scenario B closes 11, with attendance boundary changes, program moves, and grade-level changes at dozens more. Every Scenario B closure is also in Scenario A. Neither is final: Superintendent Armstrong recommends a plan in November and the board votes in December.

PPS has also described how it is choosing. Its targets are about 360 students for a K-5 school and 500 for a K-8 or middle school. When two schools combine, it favors the building in better condition. It also weighs operating costs, earthquake risk, travel distance, keeping classmates together, possible consolidation of immersion programs, and a review under its racial equity lens. PPS says no single measure, including utilization, decides the outcome.

This explorer gathers data to visualize these factors for every in-scope PPS school. Academic performance is included for context, though it is notably absent from the criteria PPS has discussed publicly.

Why this matters

Current utilization is a 2025 snapshot; closure decisions play out over 2027-2030+. PRC's 10-year forecast is the best public per-school answer to "how empty will this building be by then?" Schools where 2025 enrollment looks defensible but 2035 enrollment does not are the ones most exposed by a forward-looking analysis. Sellwood (-33%), Hosford (-30%), Metropolitan Learning Center (-30%), and Lewis (-29%) top that list.

Housing growth forecast (Metro BLI, ~2035)

Projected new residential units inside each school's PPS attendance area, from Metro's 2045 Building Land Inventory (BLI) Housing-Employment Allocation grid. It's an area-weighted sum over intersecting BLI cells; schools without a published catchment (focus-option, alternative) fall back to a 1-mile buffer (shown in teal). Each bar below is one school, ranked by forecast growth. Source: Portland/Metro BLI Housing-Employment Allocation.

How to read this

BLI is Metro's long-horizon forecast of where new housing will land by ~2035 under current zoning and policy. It's useful for spotting which catchments are expected to absorb disproportionate growth. A school at the high end of the distribution sits in a catchment projected to absorb a large share of new housing, so closing one there risks leaving a growth area without capacity by the time the forecast units arrive.

More info on this dataset

Holmes Engineering was engaged by PPS in 2023 to update the district's seismic assessment for each school. Field walkthroughs in 2023-2024 produced two rough-order-of-magnitude (ROM) cost estimates per school: a URM-only partial retrofit (scoped to just the unreinforced-masonry areas) and a complete retrofit covering every school building. Costs use representative $/SF from recent schematic-design pricing and are engineer's estimates for initial budgeting only.

Fifteen schools appear with $0 remaining. Holmes excluded them because they had recently completed modernizations, had in-progress bond-funded retrofits, or were newly constructed to modern benchmark codes. Nine additional schools have 2025 bond funding. Two (Beverly Cleary and Rose City Park) have full modernizations scheduled and show as solid blue bars. The other seven (Vernon, Winterhaven, Ainsworth, Beach, Capitol Hill, Kelly, Richmond) have a URM-only partial retrofit scheduled. The bond covers the unreinforced-masonry scope but not a complete retrofit. Those bars are split: blue for the funded URM-only portion, red for the remaining gap to a complete retrofit.

For the remaining ~60 schools with fully-red bars, Holmes's 2024 complete-retrofit estimate is not in any current or approved bond. The • marker indicates that some earlier bond money was spent on targeted scope (typically roofs, URM bracing, or partial structural upgrades). That past work reduces seismic risk but does not fund the remaining Holmes estimate. A school without the • marker has no recorded bond retrofit history at all.

Caveats: treat the Holmes numbers as order-of-magnitude. URM classification comes from Holmes's 2024 URM database. Bars rank by the complete-retrofit total (blue + red for split bars), so URM and non-URM schools share one axis. Funded vs. unfunded is derived from the PPS bond page and reflects the publicly announced 2025 bond scope as of 2026-04.

Why this matters for closure decisions

Total enrollment at a shared-building school blends two distinct populations. The DLI strand pulls students district-wide by lottery, so those seats are not representative of neighborhood demand, and the mainstream cohort, which is the actual catchment-based enrollment, can be a fraction of the posted total. Closing one of these buildings affects two different constituencies: the catchment families who lose their neighborhood school, and the lottery families across the district who lose a DLI option. PPS has named "access to programs" as one of its four closure-decision factors; consolidating a DLI host also removes that program from its current neighborhood unless the strand is explicitly relocated.

Methodology & sources

Each of the 74 rows is one PPS school in the closure-announcement scope (elementary, K-8, middle, and alternative; PPS's 9 high schools are excluded per the March 10 announcement). Columns are pulled from the sources below and joined on a mix of NCES school ID, Oregon ODE School Institution ID, and manual name mapping for recent renamings (e.g., Madison → McDaniel, Wilson → Wells-Barnett, Fernwood → Beverly Cleary). Geographic indicators (affordable housing, permits) are aggregated to each school using its actual PPS attendance boundary, with a 1-mile haversine radius as a fallback for focus-option / alternative / embedded programs that don't have a published catchment.

How PPS computes utilization (and why two numbers can differ)

Utilization is enrollment divided by a building's functional capacity. PPS builds that capacity in three steps. First, gross capacity: every classroom of 500 sq ft or more is bracketed by size and assigned a student count (under 800 sq ft → 24 students, 800–1000 → 27, over 1000 → 30), then summed. Second, room set-asides are subtracted as student stations, not as a rate change: each SPED or deaf/hard-of-hearing focus room removes 12 stations, and each Early Childhood room, Learning Center, gym, computer lab, co-located program, art/music room, and leased space removes 27. Third, a utilization rate multiplies what remains: 100% at K-5 and K-8, 85% at middle and high schools, minus five percentage points each for Title I, TSI, and CSI status. So the Special Ed Learning Center, Early Childhood, and leased spaces in the records request lower capacity by removing rooms up front; they do not change the percentage rate.

The workbook PPS released has its reduction inputs (SPED and Early Childhood room counts, Title I / TSI / CSI flags) pulled from an external linked file that was not included, so those cells and the final functional-capacity column read #REF!. The number still can't be reproduced from the file alone. We recovered PPS's actual functional-capacity denominator a different way: the workbook cached each school's enrollment and utilization for the years when the model was intact, and since utilization is just enrollment divided by that fixed denominator, enrollment ÷ utilization returns it exactly. The back-out agrees across three years for nearly every school.

This recovered figure and the 2021 LRFP number are both PPS's own, and for 64 of the 74 schools they match. For 10 schools they differ by 5% or more, usually because of a scoping choice rather than an error. Creston is the starkest: the operational model excludes its annex building, roughly halving its capacity and moving its utilization from about 39% to about 78%. Those 10 rows carry a ⚠ marker next to their utilization in the table, with both PPS figures on hover. The table and charts keep the LRFP number as the displayed value so nothing re-ranks on the contested figure; the flag is there so a reader can see where PPS's own sources disagree.

Building inventory, leases & sprinklers

The same workbook carries three other things the dashboard now surfaces, all recovered per building and shown in the table:

  • Gross capacity and room inventory. Summed from the workbook's room-level classroom list (every space of 500+ sq ft, with its square footage and use type). Gross capacity is the physical ceiling before any set-asides; the gap between it and functional capacity is exactly the SPED, Early Childhood, art/music, computer, gym, co-located, and leased rooms PPS holds back, plus the utilization rate. These counts are 2026-current, so they reflect building changes (portable removals, the Benson re-measure) that never reached the older functional-capacity figures. Hover the gross-capacity cell for the room-type breakdown and average classroom size.
  • Leased classrooms. Rooms rented to outside tenants (Head Start, preschool programs, health clinics, county and regional programs). They are subtracted from usable capacity, so a school can read as underused while renting space out. Hover for the tenant list.
  • Fire-sprinkler status. Most older PPS buildings are not fully sprinklered; many run under a K-2 program that grants a phased sprinkler exception with an expiration year. This is a building-safety axis alongside the seismic data above. Hover for the K-2 status and notes.

Ventilation & indoor air

PPS Indoor Air Quality: airflow survey (2021)
NEBB-certified per-room airflow tests (Amerseco + Neudorfer Engineers, 2021) published as one PDF per building. Measured total supply CFM, outside-air CFM, and derived ASHRAE Total Effective Air Changes per Hour (ACH_e) with and without portable filtration. We parse each PDF for room-level readings, then report per-building: rooms tested, median ACH_e without portable filters (the HVAC-only floor), the share of rooms below Lancet's 3 ACH lower bound and 6 ACH recommended classroom target, and whether a MERV-13 filter upgrade had been installed at test time. Coverage: 76 of 83 roster buildings; omitted schools (Benson, McDaniel, Kellogg, Alliance, ACCESS, Bridger Creative Science, Clark) were either newly-modernized after the survey or don't have a standalone building report.

Enrollment forecast (PRC, 10-year)

PSU Population Research Center: Portland Public Schools Enrollment Forecasts 2026-27 to 2035-36 (July 2026)
Per-school 10-year enrollment projection parsed from Table 5.5 (pages 37-40) of PRC's annual forecast report. PRC uses a cohort-component model: Grade Progression Ratios (GPR) extrapolate existing cohorts forward; Kindergarten entry is modeled as age-eligible-population × capture rate with Lee-Carter fertility/mortality trends and an autoregressive migration model. The per-school table is published in the medium scenario only; Low and High are published at the district-and-grade level (Tables 5.3 and 5.4). We populate each school's 2026-27 through 2035-36 medium forecast, derive a 10-year percent change from PRC's own 2025-26 historic baseline (ODE reports Hayhurst and Odyssey together; PRC splits them), and compute a forward-looking utilization = 2035-36 forecast ÷ 2021 functional capacity. Alliance High School is the only in-scope school PRC does not forecast.
Low/high scenario bands (derived)
PRC does not publish per-school low/high, so each school's scenario band is estimated by scaling the medium forecast by the district-wide Low/Medium and High/Medium ratios at its grade band. For 2035-36: K-5 × 0.93 (low) / × 1.11 (high); 6-8 × 0.90 (low) / × 1.07 (high). Middle schools have narrower bands because those cohorts are already in the pipeline; elementaries depend on K/1 recovery, the biggest scenario lever.

Regional context (not attached per-school)

Metro Council's 2045 Distributed Forecast (Ord. 21-1457, adopted Feb 2021) projects City of Portland growing from 668,429 to 774,219 residents (+16%) by 2045, and unincorporated Multnomah County from 19,328 to 40,490 (+110%). Metro only publishes this forecast at city/county level (no tract or TAZ breakdown, and no age bands), so it is not attached to individual schools here; it is background only. PSU Population Research Center's report covers PPS specifically (and is the source of the 10-year enrollment forecast above), but does not forecast Multnomah County population, so Metro is the only public long-horizon population forecast for the PPS footprint.

Rightsizing scenarios (PPS, October 2026)

Parsed from PPS's two-page scenario comparison (footer dated October 4, 2026, released October 5). The district-wide measures are PPS's own projections for 2031-32 and are shown as published. School-level changes come from PPS's lists of closures, attendance boundary changes, program moves, and grade-level changes; the parser checks its counts against the totals PPS prints beside each list. Derived metrics

Student-experience metrics (CRDC 2021)
PPS doesn't publish a per-school "student experience" composite, and the obvious post-COVID source (Oregon's At-A-Glance "regular attenders %" and teacher-experience tables) isn't available as a clean bulk download. As a stand-in, three 2020-21 CRDC-derived rates are surfaced: support staff per 100 students (counselor + social worker + psychologist FTE ÷ 2020-21 enrollment × 100), chronic absenteeism rate (students chronically absent ÷ 2020-21 enrollment), and OSS per 100 (out-of-school suspension instances ÷ 2020-21 enrollment × 100; counts events, not unique students). Caveat: 2020-21 was the second pandemic year; chronic-absence rates were unusually high district-wide and should be read as a floor on current rates.
Transportation impact (nearest same-level alternative)
For the 20 lowest-enrollment in-scope schools by 2025-26 enrollment, we estimate how far families would have to travel if their school closed. Each school is measured to the nearest neighborhood school of the same grade band (elementary, K-8, middle, alternative) using road-network driving distance from the OSRM routing engine, not straight-line distance: we rank candidate destinations by great-circle distance over NCES CCD coordinates, request driving distance and free-flow drive time for the closest five, and keep the minimum (falling back to great-circle distance if a route request fails). Each bar is an independent “if this one school closed” estimate. Every other neighborhood school stays a valid destination, since PPS would realistically close only a handful, not all 20, so we do not assume the others close. Focus-option / lottery schools (Buckman, Winterhaven, Odyssey, Bridger, Sunnyside Environmental, MLC, ACCESS) are excluded both as candidates and as destinations: they admit district-wide by lottery, not by catchment, so a nearest-neighborhood distance is not a meaningful reassignment. Caveats: drive time is free-flow (excludes traffic and pickup/dropoff), schools with no same-level non-lottery peer (e.g. Alliance) are omitted, and actual PPS reassignment would follow redrawn boundaries that may differ.

Caveats

Functional capacity is from the 2021 Long-Range Facility Plan and does not reflect post-2021 bond work or reconfigurations (Harrison Park's conversion to a middle school, Lincoln's rebuild, etc.). Building square footage is from 2009 and may understate capacity at schools expanded under recent bonds. CRDC LEP/IDEA counts are from 2020 (COVID year) and are divided by 2025-26 enrollment to derive shares; treat as approximate. Free/reduced meal shares use 2022 counts over 2022 enrollment; post-2022 trends are not reflected. State and federal data report Odyssey's students under Hayhurst, although Odyssey moved to the East Sylvan building in fall 2016. Enrollment and utilization for both use PPS's own split. Hayhurst's other state-reported figures still include Odyssey. Permits represent approvals, not completions; a permit may be issued but not built. PRC per-school forecasts reflect spring 2026 assumptions (net migration, K/1 capture rate, birth rates). PRC reports district-level forecast errors only (no per-school error analysis): the 2025 one-year mean absolute percent error across all grades was 1.37% (Table 4.3), but grade-level error was largest in Kindergarten (3.8% underestimate). Pandemic-era multi-year forecasts missed by up to 8-20% over 4-5 years (Table 4.4). Treat per-school medium forecasts as a central estimate with scenario uncertainty that is larger at the elementary level (K/1 is the biggest lever).
